# Standing editing preferences

Give patient establishing shots, reactions and spontaneous exchanges room to finish. Preserve characteristic location sound. Let music yield to dialogue. Use a title or map when it helps the viewer understand where or when something happens.

Keep a spoken phrase on a continuous audio track when cutting away to a reaction or detail. Return on a natural pause or gesture. When the audio itself needs a cut, preserve meaning, check breaths and match room tone. Listen across both joins with the screen off.

Check uncertain speech by ear and moving crops in motion. For reels, build around one clear moment. For longer films, organize chapters around the story. Add project-specific feedback here only when the user wants it applied to future edits.

## Pacing and transitions for vlogs

Apply these defaults when the project brief and rushes support them.

- Open with a concrete action, encounter or glimpse of the place. Get into the first scene early. Add a brief title over a useful shot when it helps orient the viewer.
- Vary the pace within each chapter. Compress routine movement and setup into a short sequence of distinct images. A wide shot can establish the place, a detail can show what is happening, and a face or reaction can show why it matters. Settle into a longer stretch when an exchange, action or reflection earns the time.
- Cut directly by default. When the footage supports it, cut on a continuing gesture, carry the outgoing location sound over the first shot of the next scene, or let the incoming sound lead into the picture change. Keep speech intact across cutaways. Give a real change of time or place an arrival image and use a short location or day label only when the image needs help.
- Build the rhythm around dialogue, gestures and location sound. Use music accents selectively to support a montage or scene change. Leave space after a reveal, punchline or candid reaction, and let a strong closing image or reflection finish.
- During selects, mark the opening moment, a sequence that can be compressed, a moment worth holding, and one or two scene changes with usable sound or movement. Check those choices in the pilot when the project calls for one. Review joins in motion with sound, and shorten any sequence that repeats information.

Never cut someone off mid-sentence, including at the start of a shot or under music. Keep the full thought and its response through a natural pause. If the picture runs long, cut the picture first and carry the voice across. If a recording itself interrupts the phrase, choose another complete exchange. Check both ends of every spoken selection before delivery.
